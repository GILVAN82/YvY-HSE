# Control of Hazardous Energy (Lockout/Tagout)

## Purpose and scope
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 addresses hazardous energy during servicing and maintenance when unexpected energization, start-up, or release of stored energy could injure workers. It requires an energy-control program built around procedures, training, and periodic inspection. Lockout is used where the isolating device can accept a lock; tagout is subject to additional protection requirements.

## Core control sequence
The procedure should cover preparation for shutdown, equipment shutdown, isolation of energy sources, application of lockout/tagout devices, control of stored or residual energy, and verification of isolation before work starts. Before return to service, the work area and personnel are checked, devices are removed under controlled rules, and affected employees are notified.

## Program elements
The program should define responsibilities, suitable lock/tag hardware, employee training and retraining, periodic inspection, and arrangements for contractors, group work, and shift changes when relevant.
