# Permit-to-Work Systems

## Purpose
HSE describes permit-to-work (PTW) as a formal, recorded control process for work identified as potentially hazardous. A permit does not make a task safe by itself; it sits within a safe system of work and communicates the job, hazards, limitations, precautions, responsible people, and authorized time period.

## Essential features
A robust PTW system defines who may authorize work and the limits of that authority, connects the permit to risk assessment and required precautions, and controls issue, acceptance, suspension, handover, hand-back, and closure. It should address interacting work, isolation, competence, training, supervision, monitoring, audit, and review.

## Operational principle
PTW is intended for work where risk or complexity justifies formal control. Required precautions should be identified before work starts and verified in practice. Equipment should return to normal service only after controlled hand-back and readiness checks.

## YVY HSE RAG handling notes
- Use this document for reusable PTW-system knowledge: purpose, lifecycle, authorization, interfaces with risk assessment, isolation, competence, handover and monitoring.
- Do not use it to determine whether a specific permit is open, expired, suspended, or signed; those are SQL/tool questions.
- HSG250 is UK guidance and is particularly oriented to petroleum, chemical and allied industries, although its control principles can be useful more broadly.
